Beta phase

The Beta phase is the third stage of the GDS delivery lifecycle. It focuses on the service moving from ideas and prototypes into a working, usable product. It builds on learnings from previous phases, and prepares the service for real-world use and eventual Live release.

The duration of a Beta phase varies, but typically lasts several months. Commonly, the phase can last 3-6 months or 6-12 months. Engagements can be shorter or longer, depending on the complexity of the service.

There are two phases within Beta:

  • Private Beta: a limited release to a sample group of real users to test the service in a controlled way
  • Public Beta: open to the public and often promoted more widely to get broader user feedback

The assessment can occur at either end of Private Beta (before going Public Beta), or end of Public Beta (before going Live).

The purpose of Beta is to check if:

  • the service meets the 14 points of the GDS Service Standard
  • user needs are being met effectively through research, design and technology
  • the team is prepared for scaling the service
  • the service is secure, accessible and sustainable
  • plans are in place for continuous improvement

Features

Your Beta phase will:

  • show evidence of user research and how findings have shaped the design of the service
  • be a simple, accessible design, consistent with GOV.UK and NHS design manual patterns
  • be open code, secure, maintainable and cloud ready
  • use a multidisciplinary team and agile ways of working in line with the GS framework
  • measure performance through metrics, KPIs and feedback
  • support users who can't use the online service, through online and assisted digital routes
  • comply with GDPR around data collection, protection and usage
  • show operations readiness, including supporting channels, responding to incidents, and changes in size or scale
  • plan to move into the Live phase

Tip: User research should continue to run as usual in parallel with the pilot.

Benefits

There are many benefits to the Beta stage, including:

  • ensuring compliance with the service standard
  • it provides an independent expert review
  • it improves service quality
  • it serves as a checkpoint to avoid a “big bang” release
  • it encourages agile best practice
  • it helps avoid costly mistakes
  • it ensures public digital services are built to a unified standard

What the GDS Assessment Panel is looking for

Assessors want to see evidence that:

  • You have a functional version of the service that can handle real user interactions, not just prototypes.
  • You've created a working end-to-end service including both online and offline routes.
  • You have real integrations with back-end systems.
  • Technical decisions have been validated through real use.
  • Your infrastructure is ready to scale.

Artefacts to produce

You should make sure you include:

  • evidence that all user groups identified have been spoken to
  • working Beta service pilot data
  • deployed environments used in private beta
  • integrated components used in private beta, including APIs, identity and data flows
  • usability testing plans and reports
  • accessibility testing outputs
  • research findings summaries
  • updated personas
  • refined interaction prototype journey flows
  • refined user journey maps
  • refined to-be service blueprints
  • evidence of iterations following user feedback and pilot feedback
  • technical architecture documentation
  • infrastructure diagrams
  • integration and API documentation
  • deployment pipelines and CI/CD configurations
  • technical decision logs
  • code repositories
  • security model or security approach document
  • penetration testing reports
  • vulnerability assessments
  • data protection impact assessment, if applicable
  • incident management procedures
  • performance metrics, including CsAT
  • early analytics data, including completion rates, errors and drop-offs
  • operational and service-level indicators (SLIs/SLOs)
  • evidence of iteration based on performance data
  • backlog of changes
  • evidence linking user insight to change and impact
  • updated Beta roadmap
  • WCAG 2.1 AA audit results
  • comms plan or toolkit
  • pilot plan and data results
  • evidence naming of the service in line with GDS and research

Tip: Have the training needs and plans for frontline staff who support the service been clearly identified?
