Setting up your team

Setting up your service team for a Government Digital Service (GDS) assessment is key to demonstrating that you have the right skills, roles, and ways of working in place. Assessors want to see a capable, collaborative, multidisciplinary team that meets the GDS Service Standard.

Gather a multidisciplinary team

GDS expects service teams to have all the skills needed to design, build, and run a service from end to end. If these skills are not in place, assessors may decide your service does not meet the standard. In some cases, this can lead to a fail.

You should have access to the following roles or skills:

  • product owner - owns the vision, prioritises work, and works with stakeholders
  • delivery manager - makes sure the team can deliver and removes any blockers
  • user researcher - gathers insights, tests prototypes, and validates assumptions
  • interaction designer - designs user journeys, wireframes, and prototypes
  • service designer - designs the end-to-end journey of a service, as well as service blueprints and user journey maps for online and offline routes 
  • content designer - creates clear, user-focused content that meets GDS standards
  • developer - builds, tests and deploys the service across frontend, backend, and DevOps
  • business analyst - maps processes and makes sure the product meets its goals and requirements
  • security and privacy lead - responsible for data protection and security compliance
  • performance analyst - monitors service performance, and service continuity

You'll need different skills at different stages of your service. Read guidance on GOV.UK about the roles you’ll need at each service phase.

Tip: If you cannot have all the roles full-time, you'll need to explain how you get access to these skills. For example, you might use shared specialists or external consultants.

Define roles and responsibilities

For the assessment, be ready to present:

  • who does what, with clear responsibilities
  • how decisions are made, such as product decisions or design choices
  • how the team works together, including daily stand-ups, retrospectives and sprint planning

Demonstrate working in an agile, collaborative way

Assessment panels expect:

  • regular iterations - evidence that you work in sprints, release often, and adapt based on feedback
  • user involvement - examples of how the team works closely with users throughout delivery
  • cross-functional collaboration - designers, developers and researchers working together, not in silos
  • transparency - visible ways of working, such as using tools like Jira, Trello or GitHub to track work

Show strong leadership and governance

Assessment panels expect to see:

  • how the product owner or service owner provides direction and sets a clear vision
  • clear escalation routes and decision-making governance
  • evidence of stakeholder engagement and sponsorship
  • how risks are managed, identified and mitigated
  • what limits the service, such as policy and business decisions, and how you manage those limits
  • each discipline presenting and answering questions directly - it's a concern if only one person speaks for the whole team

Prepare your team for questions

Assessors will ask team members questions based on their area of expertise. You and your team should be ready to answer:

  • how do you incorporate user research and design into your work?
  • how do you ensure the service is accessible and inclusive?
  • how do you manage technical debt and security?
  • what tools do you use for continuous delivery and testing?
  • how does your team handle feedback and improvement?
  • how does the team challenge or work within policy limits?

You need to show your team meets each Service Standard. Evidence can come from different areas of work. For example:

  • design and research work is used to show standards 1 to 5 and 8
  • ways of working as a team, including agile, is used to show standards 6 and 7
  • technical and architectural work is used to show standards 9 to 14

Tip: Simple visuals help assessors understand the team structure, responsibilities, and decision-making quickly.
