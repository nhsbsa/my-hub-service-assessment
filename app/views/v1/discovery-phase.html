Discovery phase

A service will not be officially assessed during the Discovery phase, but it is important to complete Discovery before moving to Alpha.

You may be asked by DHSC to present a Discovery review and are advised to ask our in-house trained assessors to conduct a mock Discovery review. These reviews help ensure your service is ready to move into Alpha.

DHSC are currently exploring the value of Discovery reviews. If you'd like your service to have one, contact our in-house assessors.

Preparing for Discovery is about showing that you've explored the problem space and are ready to move into Alpha.

You should have a clear understanding of:

  • the entire problem space your service sits in
  • the problem your service will directly solve
  • the user needs of those who will use your service
  • the context in which the service operates
  • the risks and opportunities
  • the solution options you've considered
  • your plan for Alpha

Features

Your Discovery phase will:

  • include qualitative and quantitative research methods to understand the context and problem
  • include research reports, as-is service blueprints, as-is user journeys and personas
  • include research into accessibility, inclusivity and access needs
  • typically take 6 to 12 weeks
  • be delivered by a multidisciplinary team
  • identify context, pain points and opportunities
  • explore constraints such as legacy technology and legislation
  • provide a go/no-go recommendation for Alpha
  • include a plan for Alpha if recommending a go decision
  • be more cost-effective than going straight into delivery

Benefits

There are many benefits to conducting a Discovery phase, including:

  • it forces you to define the problem
  • you'll carry out research with a range of users
  • it helps establish whether there's a need to continue to Alpha
  • it is a crucial part of the GDS service standard process
  • it helps you understand the context around the problem
  • it allows you to define a vision for the service
  • it helps you understand inclusivity and access needs
  • it helps you understand constraints such as legacy technology and legislation
  • you'll identify measures of success
  • it enables you to identify opportunities

What the GDS assessment panel is looking for

Assessors want to see evidence that:

  • you understand the problem and the users
  • you're not jumping straight to solutions
  • you've gathered a wide range of insights, not just from stakeholders
  • you've explored multiple approaches
  • you have a credible plan for Alpha
  • the service is worth the public funds invested in it

Artefacts to produce

You should make sure you include:

  • research plans
  • user interviews and user research playbacks with insights
  • as-is user journey maps and service blueprints
  • a problem statement
  • the policy behind the service
  • a robust plan for Alpha
  • rough design concepts proposing a range of possible solutions
