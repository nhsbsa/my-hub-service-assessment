Discovery phase

A service will not be officially assessed on the Discovery phase, but it’s important to explore it before moving to Alpha. You should ask our inhouse trained assessors to do a discovery review, to ensure your service is ready to move to Alpha.

SC are currently exploring the value of Discovery reviews. If you'd like your service to have one, contact our inhouse assessors.

Preparing for a Discovery phase is all about showing that you've explored the problem space and are ready to move into Alpha.

You should have a clear understanding of:

  • the entire problem space your service sits in
  • the problem your service will directly solve
  • the user needs of those who will use your service
  • the context in which the service operates
  • the risks and opportunities
  • the solution options you've considered
  • your solid plan for Alpha

Features

Your discovery phase will:

  • include qualitative and quantitative research methods to understand context and problem
  • include research reports, as-is service blueprints, as-is user journeys, personas
  • include research with access needs and inclusivity
  • typically take 6 to 12 weeks
  • be formed of a multidisciplinary team
  • identify context, pain points, and opportunities
  • explore constraints such as legacy tech and legislation
  • provide a go/ no go for Alpha
  • include a plan for Alpha, if providing a go
  • be more cost effective than going straight to build

Benefits

There are many benefits to conducting a discovery phase, including:

  • it forces you to define the problem
  • you’ll carry out research with a range of users
  • it helps establish if there's a need to continue to Alpha
  • it is crucial in the GDS process
  • it will help you understand context around the problem
  • it allows us to define a vision for the service
  • it will help you understand inclusivity and access needs
  • it will help you understand constraints such as legacy technology and legislation
  • you’ll identify measures of success
  • it enables understanding of opportunities

What the GDS Assessment Panel is looking for

Assessors want to see evidence that:

  • You understand the problem and the users.
  • You’re not jumping to solutions.
  • You’ve gathered a wide range of insights, not just from stakeholders.
  • You’ve explored multiple approaches.
  • You have a credible plan for Alpha.
  • The service is worth the public funds invested in it.

Artefacts to produce

You should make sure you include:

  • research plans
  • user interviews and UR play backs with insights
  • user journeys
  • problem statement
  • policy behind the service
  • a robust plan for alpha
  • rough design ideas proposing a few solutions
